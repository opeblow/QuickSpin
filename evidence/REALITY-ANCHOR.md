# Reality Anchor — QuickSpin

Verification status: **VERIFIED AGAINST PRIMARY SOURCES** on 2026-09-15.

Canonical pattern:

`SIGNAL / OPPORTUNITY → REAL NEGATIVE EVENT → OBSERVABLE IMPACT → DESIGN LESSON → MITIGATION`

This document is intentionally narrower than a market thesis. It proves that the product is rooted in an observed reality and makes explicit what QuickSpin can and cannot mitigate.

## 1. Signal positif / opportunité — why this deserves to be built

AI applications routinely impose non-zero waits while reasoning, retrieving, calling tools, or assembling outputs. A 2026 HCI experiment with **425 participants** studied **10-, 30-, and 60-second** system-imposed delays. It found that temporal feedback design changes perceived wait, frustration, and ambiguity: no time display made waits feel longer and more ambiguous, while different feedback modes changed frustration.

Primary research source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04138

**Opportunity:** the useful product space is not “pretend latency disappeared.” It is to make unavoidable wait states more legible, interactive, measurable, and honest about uncertainty/outcome.

## 2. Événement négatif concret, réel et vérifiable

**June 2–3, 2026 — OpenAI: “Elevated error rates on Codex, ChatGPT and Responses API.”**

OpenAI’s official incident write-up states that approximately **9:46 PM PDT to 12:01 AM PDT** some customers experienced elevated errors and latency across several services. For affected Responses API traffic, requests took longer than normal to begin generating. Codex requests were incorrectly rejected with **HTTP 429** responses. ChatGPT login, authentication and conversation flows experienced failures/partial degradation.

Primary incident source: https://status.openai.com/incidents/01KT5XJ5ATD6RMYP908WS69FVD/write-up

This is a real production event from the provider’s own status record. It is not a hypothetical risk and it is not generated by QuickSpin.

**Honesty boundary:** QuickSpin does **not** claim it would have prevented, shortened, or repaired this provider incident.

## 3. Impact observable

Observed impact from the incident itself:

- increased time before affected Responses API requests began generating;
- unexpected Codex request rejection via HTTP 429;
- failed/degraded ChatGPT login, authentication and conversation flows;
- incident conditions persisted across roughly a **2 hour 15 minute** window for the affected service set before recovery was reported.

The HCI source above independently establishes why wait-state presentation matters to user experience even when the underlying provider eventually succeeds.

**Product-relevant impact class:** delay, rejection, broken continuity, uncertainty about whether work is still progressing, and risk of a waiting UI implying success that may never arrive.

## 4. Leçon / implication design

The real incident and wait research imply non-negotiable design rules:

1. A waiting UI must never equate **“still waiting”** with **“will definitely succeed.”**
2. Unknown progress must stay indeterminate; fake percentages are disallowed.
3. Execution claims require host-owned provenance; UI animation alone is not proof that a tool/retrieval/artifact event occurred.
4. Failure and cancellation must terminate differently from completion.
5. A failed provider/request path must not fabricate an AI answer.
6. Perceived-wait measurement must allow a negative result: the wait may feel equal or longer.
7. Missing evidence must produce **UNKNOWN / abstention**, not a confident gameplay mutation.

## 5. Réponse / mitigation QuickSpin

QuickSpin mitigates the **waiting-experience and evidence-integrity problem**, not provider reliability itself:

- **650 ms anti-flash threshold** so fast responses do not flash a game unnecessarily;
- **phase-aware indeterminate waiting** instead of fabricated percent-complete progress;
- **evidence-bearing execution signals** for retrieval/tool/artifact/warning events;
- `signal-rejected → UNKNOWN / INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` when provenance is absent or invalid;
- explicit `completed`, `cancelled`, and `failed` terminal outcomes;
- failed sessions remain persisted with failure code/message and no fabricated response;
- signed **Wait Receipt** can report shorter, equal, or longer perceived wait;
- controlled negative-path demo performs an actual rejected Promise and ends in failure rather than success.
